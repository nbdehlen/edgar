Yes on expiry. For a full cache, it removes the **least recently used company**, which isn’t necessarily the company cached longest ago.

There are two separate caches:

- **Ticker mapping:** the entire ticker-to-CIK mapping is one cached entry, with `tickerTtlMs`.
- **Company submissions:** one entry per CIK, with `submissionsTtlMs`, up to `maxCompanies`. GOOG and GOOGL therefore share an entry.

When a company is requested:

1. **Cached and still valid:** return its data and mark it as most recently used. This does **not** extend its TTL.
2. **Cached but expired:** remove that entry and fetch fresh data. Expiry is checked on access; there’s no background cleanup.
3. **Not cached:** fetch its data.
4. **Already being fetched:** share the existing request rather than start another.
5. **Fetch succeeds:** cache the result, starting its TTL from that moment.
6. **Fetch fails:** return the error. No failure or expired data is cached.

When the cache is full, eviction happens **after the new company loads successfully**. For example, with capacity three:

```text
Least recently used → Most recently used
AAPL, JPM, SPOT

Request AAPL:
JPM, SPOT, AAPL

Load MSFT successfully:
SPOT, AAPL, MSFT       ← JPM removed
```

If MSFT fails to load, the existing entries stay.

One subtlety: when making room, the cache doesn’t search for expired entries first—it simply removes the least recently used entry. An expired entry elsewhere can remain until it’s requested or eventually evicted.
